# Reproduction Delivery Contract

Load only for an explicitly requested reproducible exemplar or replayable package.
This is the shared handoff contract, not an analysis method, experiment design,
backup system, new manifest or mandatory homepage. Existing object-specific formats,
identities, receipts and write boundaries remain authoritative.

## Required delivery content

Keep a short human-readable replay note or existing package guide that identifies
the selected object and links to its separate machine records. Reuse existing
filenames and receipts; no additional inventory/schema is required. The delivery
must make these six items independently inspectable:

1. **Inputs and source identity:** selected object identities, source versions/hashes
   and required non-secret environment capabilities. For non-redistributable PDFs,
   keep the Zotero attachment identity and acquisition prerequisites, not a copied
   private PDF, credential or personal host path.
2. **Product and interface:** applicable template/schema versions, actual installed
   product version, and the public command or installed skill entry with its inputs.
   A development renderer or a cached success receipt is not a new installed replay.
3. **Expected result:** filenames, primary/supporting ownership, required sections or
   fields, relationships and visible format. The owning format contract controls the
   details; an example's paper facts or contribution counts are not a generic template.
4. **Replay and effects:** chosen new destination, reproducible prerequisites and
   operation entry, files/state it may create or change, and conflict/stop behavior.
   State whether this is regeneration/execution, archive restoration or an idempotent
   lookup. An existing-result copy does not prove new execution. Do not overwrite an
   existing destination or expand permission to other objects, services or collections.
5. **Observed acceptance:** separate automatic results from human assessment, with
   the exact tested object/version and actual artifacts. When a person must judge
   appearance, editability or navigation, identify the object, opening action,
   expected visible effect and pending decision in the conversation as well as the
   record. Installation, static links and conformance do not replace that assessment.
6. **Limits and failures:** preserve missing sources, unsupported capabilities,
   conflicts, failed attempts and unverified checks. Say which requirement is still
   incomplete. A recovery snapshot is not verified backup, and a replayable package
   does not by itself establish scientific support or universal robustness.

Reproduction means the declared inputs and result contract can be replayed. It does
not require identical internal reasoning or byte-identical model-authored prose.
Deterministic outputs or preserved archive bytes follow their specific contracts.
Keep machine hashes/identities and receipts separate from the readable body while
retaining the links needed to inspect them.

## Capability ownership

- Paper analysis: `skills/analyze-paper/references/reproduction.md` owns versioned
  requests, staged versus canonical pairs and paper-specific conformance. The selected
  analysis template still owns Markdown, editable Canvas, evidence and source images.
- Knowledge Source archive: `references/knowledge-reproduction.md` owns export,
  restore and ownership preservation, not regeneration or reader success.
- Project/experiment: `skills/init-project/references/reproducibility.md` owns layout
  and actual replay; project identity and Run/Attempt/Target/Artifact remain in their
  existing records. Organizing or comparing records does not execute them.
- Native opening: `skills/find-resource/references/resource-location.md` owns tool
  prerequisites and requests. A window request is distinct from visible reader behavior.

Shared presentation and authority boundaries remain in `human-presentation.md`,
`storage-policy.md` and the relevant source/security policy. Do not turn this handoff
into a second owner store or silently synchronize independent objects.
